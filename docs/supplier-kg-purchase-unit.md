# Kilogram supplier purchase units

Baseline: main `74affa19c69a9518a45e7eb551437cd68b2ac014` (PR #244 merged), 14 October 2026. Branch: `codex/supplier-kg-purchase-unit-20261014`. No production mutation, paid provider call, merge or deployment.

`kg` is now a canonical supplier purchase unit in the product form suggestions, AI review and supplier mapping selectors. KG/kilogram source labels normalize to `kg`. It represents a measured purchasing quantity: one purchase unit is one kilogram, represented as either 1 kg or 1000 g. It does not establish the mass of an individual piece. A 5 kg box remains a separately named package, not a kg purchase unit containing 5 kg.

The shared `purchaseUnitMeasures.json` contract supplies server and client semantics. Product mutations, additional options, ordering and inventory terms reject incompatible or incorrectly scaled kg conversions. Existing snapshots are not rewritten. An old incorrectly configured kg offer must be reviewed before another order; its financial/inventory history remains intact.

AI normalization recognizes KG in the verified purchase-package column, including the split export whose inner content is ST. Its kg measure uses mass rather than interpreting ST as a mass-to-item conversion. Gram-based offers retain grams with 1000 g per kg. Named piece weights never generate inferred piece counts for these offers. Explicit per-kg prices retain their mass price channel and tax/currency contract. Missing prices and annual/statistical spend remain unknown.

Cart kg quantities support the server's three-decimal purchase precision, e.g. 0.25 kg. The quantity field commits after Enter or blur, so entering a zero prefix does not prematurely delete a line. It pins the revision when editing starts and preserves unconfirmed edits. The cart service sends numeric values from HTML fields, preserves the original UUID on unknown acknowledgments, and rejects blank input without treating it as zero. An explicit committed zero retains the existing removal behavior.

Acceptance examples: 2.5 kg at EUR 8 net/kg yields EUR 20 net goods value and 2500 g for a gram-based catalog article. Both default and additional kg options reject kg-to-piece/volume conversions and false 1 g or 2 kg contents. A 1 kg buy measure does not claim that a chicken or other individual item weighs 1 kg.
